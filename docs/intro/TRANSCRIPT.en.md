# Narration transcript

## You have a study list. What next?

You have a study list and some papers. How do you turn them into a review that people can actually check?

These two skills help prepare full texts and organize review. Use them together, or start with the step you need.

## Start with the materials you have

Bring your question, study list and existing PDFs. Include your protocol or codebook if you have one; a draft is fine.

For example: find the missing full texts in this list and preserve the IDs. Tell me exactly what to do when you need my help.

## Change routes when the evidence calls for it

The agent checks what you already have, fills metadata gaps, and tries available public, publisher or repository sources. Failed attempts stay in the log.

If you have institutional access, it can inspect library resources and access routes. A direct paywall may not be the only route.

## Make the human access step specific

Sometimes the remaining step is sign-in, authentication, or saving a file you can open. The handoff names the article, page, action and return location.

Return the file, and validation resumes. Other accessible papers can continue meanwhile. A manually saved PDF gets the same checks.

## Obtaining a file is only the first check

A readable PDF is not automatically the right report. Check the article or chapter, DOI, version and completeness, and record its pages and file hash.

If text extraction is unreliable, use another check. If identity remains uncertain, keep it pending. A filename is not identity evidence.

This stage delivers checked sources, attempt history and a remaining queue. If your full texts are already ready, start at the next stage.

## People develop the rules and choose the rounds

Before coding, the team develops the rules. You might start with a small sample, perhaps ten reports, and discuss definitions that need work.

Once the rules are usable, AI can prepare the next batch for people to verify. You can return to calibration or choose independent review.

## Choose how reviewers work

Assisted mode shows proposals and evidence. Independent mode gives reviewers the rules, sources and blank forms, without AI suggestions or other people’s feedback.

Here is the independent form in the fictional portable example. People can code first and return their answers. The team chooses the mode for each round.

## Follow one judgment back to the source

Now look at the actual Pilot workbench. The item is in the middle, with the source on the right. Click a citation to reach its page.

When a quotation matches, the reader highlights it. Then read the context and decide whether the passage supports the code.

After checking, confirm the item or choose Revise and explain the change. These demonstration responses are stored separately from research judgments.

## Give each reviewer the right task

Assign reports, fields, instructions and review modes by person. One reviewer might check methods, another concepts. The agent prepares the configuration.

Reviewers open their own package in a browser, then export a JSON file to return. They do not need an AI account or a shared login.

## People resolve differences after returns arrive

The agent checks round bindings and coverage, then organizes differences. The research team returns to the evidence and decides what to accept.

If a PDF or rule changes later, preserve the old version, identify affected work, and start the round that needs to be redone.

## Two cases show what can transfer

TALL worked with primary studies; Agency works at review level. Their units and rules differ, while source links, feedback and adjudication can transfer.

For your review, supply your own question, codebook and assignments. The cases are at different stages; they are not a controlled demonstration of time savings.

## Choose an entry point and start your first round

Both GitHub pages include installation instructions and examples. Coordinators install the skill folder in a compatible agent; reviewers can first explore a fictional demo.

With full texts and draft rules, ask: prepare review packages for our chosen sample, explain the gaps, and identify the decisions our team must make.

The aim is to reduce searching, copying and repeated navigation, while people retain control of the rules, interpretation and conclusions.
